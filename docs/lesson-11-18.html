Form the imparfait

Start with the present nous form, remove -ons, then add -ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient. The stem comes from the conjugated form, not directly from the infinitive: voir → nous voyons → voy- → il voyait. Être is the main exception: use ét-, giving j’étais, tu étais, il/elle/on était, nous étions, vous étiez, ils/elles étaient.

Subject sécher: nous séchons voir: nous voyons porter: nous portons
je séchais voyais portais
tu séchais voyais portais
il/elle/on séchait voyait portait
nous séchions voyions portions
vous séchiez voyiez portiez
ils/elles séchaient voyaient portaient

Le linge sèche rapidement → Le linge séchait rapidement — The laundry dries quickly → The laundry dried / was drying quickly. Keep the acute accent in séchait; do not copy the present singular’s è into the imperfect stem. Rappelle-toi la formule pour l’imparfait means remember the formula for the imperfect; rappelle-toi is a reflexive imperative. A verb can be pictured in a new déguisement (disguise), but the stem and ending still need to be identified separately.

For many verbs, the present nous form becomes the imperfect by inserting i before -ons: portons → portions. Check spelling changes rather than applying that shortcut mechanically. Retain y + i in voyions/voyiez, and both written i letters in étudiions/étudiiez. Check accents and consonants against the complete form; an extra letter alone does not identify a tense.

Soft g, cédille and recevoir

The spelling of manger preserves soft g /ʒ/, as in the sound of French j. In the imparfait, keep e before the endings beginning with a, but omit that extra e before -ions/-iez. This is an e, not an extra r.

Subject Present Imparfait
je mange mangeais
tu manges mangeais
il/elle/on mange mangeait
nous mangeons mangions
vous mangez mangiez
ils/elles mangent mangeaient

The “except nous and vous” reminder belongs to those imparfait spellings. Present nous mangeons needs e before o. Spelling supports pronunciation; it is part of the conjugation rule, not permission to disregard grammar.

Ordinary c normally gives /s/ before e, i, y, and /k/ before a, o, u. Use ç to retain /s/ before a, o, u: garçon, français, reçu. C + r is not a soft-c rule: crayon begins /kʁ/. Write garçon, not garcon, and keep the /s/ sound in français; English-like respellings do not reliably represent the French sounds. The name of the mark is la cédille.

Recevoir means to receive. Its present forms must be learned as an irregular pattern:

Subject Present of recevoir
je reçois
tu reçois
il/elle/on reçoit
nous recevons
vous recevez
ils/elles reçoivent

Both the singular forms and ils/elles reçoivent have ç before oi. Nous recevons / vous recevez have ordinary c before e. The past participle is reçu: Il a reçu son diplôme le mois dernier — He received his diploma last month.

Choose between imparfait and passé composé

Use the imparfait for a past situation, description, background or repeated habit, viewed as ongoing at a past reference point. Use the passé composé for an event viewed as a completed whole, a change, a decision or a step in a sequence. A past decision can have present consequences, but that is not the only use of the passé composé. Duration alone is not the test: J’ai travaillé ici pendant vingt ans presents twenty years as a completed period.

Model English Why this tense?
J’étudiais tous les jours. I studied / used to study every day. A repeated past habit.
En général, j’avais de bonnes notes. In general, I had good grades. A habitual past situation.
Le lundi, on allait à la salle de sport. On Mondays, we used to go to the gym. Repeated Mondays, rather than one identified Monday.
Hier, j’ai été au magasin. Yesterday, I went to the store. A completed visit; avoir été à is conversational here. For explicit movement, also use je suis allé(e) au magasin.
Je regardais la télévision quand le téléphone a sonné. I was watching TV when the phone rang. Ongoing background followed by an event.
J’habitais à Nice, puis j’ai déménagé à Lyon. I was living in Nice, then I moved to Lyon. A past state followed by a change.

Time expressions help interpret a sentence; they do not choose the tense by themselves:

Expression Meaning What to check
tous les jours; chaque semaine / chaque mois / chaque année every day; each week / month / year Often a repeated habit, in the present or past according to the verb and context.
en général; toujours in general; always Can describe a general or repeated situation; not restricted to the imparfait.
le lundi, le mardi… on Mondays, on Tuesdays… Often habitual. A full date can instead identify one day: le lundi 30 novembre.
hier; le mois dernier yesterday; last month Past time, with either a completed event or background: hier, il pleuvait is also possible.
ensuite; après / après ça next; after / after that Sequence or relative order; these expressions are not exclusive to past narration.
demain tomorrow Future time, which can be expressed by a present form, futur proche or futur simple.

Past participles: choose the auxiliary, then check agreement

The passé composé uses present avoir/être + past participle. For ordinary être verbs such as aller, choose allé, allée, allés, allées to match a masculine singular, feminine singular, masculine/mixed plural or feminine plural subject. Thus Il est allé au magasin, without feminine -e; Elle est allée au magasin includes it. Added agreement letters are often silent.

With avoir, the participle normally does not agree with the subject: Elle a reçu son diplôme. A preceding direct object can require agreement: les lettres que j’ai reçues. Reflexive verbs use être, but have their own agreement analysis: elles se sont lavées, elles se sont lavé les mains, elles se sont parlé.

Ils sont allés voir leur grand-mère means they went to see their grandmother. Sont allés is the passé composé of aller; the following voir stays infinitive. It is a past construction, even though it contains a second verb. For yesterday’s completed purchase, correct Hier, je vais acheter du pain to Hier, j’ai acheté du pain.

Form the futur proche with aller

Use present aller + infinitive for “be going to do something.” Conjugate aller and leave the following main verb infinitive: Je vais acheter du pain — I’m going to buy some bread; Je vais mange needs Je vais manger. This construction often presents an intention, plan or development connected with now. It can concern a later time too; neither absolute certainty nor a short delay is guaranteed by its form.

Subject Present of aller
je vais
tu vas
il/elle/on va
nous allons
vous allez
ils/elles vont

Keep the spellings vais, vas, va, allons, allez, vont: allons and allez have two l letters; allez ends in -ez. On va often means “we are going” in everyday speech and uses third-person singular conjugation. Nous allons remains valid and is common in more formal language.

Model English
Je vais commander une pizza. I’m going to order a pizza.
Tu vas prendre une douche. You’re going to take a shower.
On va partir dans cinq minutes. We’re going to leave in five minutes.
Nous allons rendre visite à mes parents. We’re going to visit my parents.
Elles vont aller au lit. They’re going to go to bed.
Vous allez nous aider. You’re going to help us.
Demain, tu vas marquer au moins un but. Tomorrow, you’re going to score at least one goal.

Two occurrences of aller can be correct: vont supplies the conjugated future construction and aller is its infinitive. In Vous allez nous aider, object nous belongs to aider, so it goes before that infinitive. The same construction works with regarder la télévision, téléphoner à quelqu’un, faire des crêpes, étudier, promener le chien, faire la lessive, chercher du travail, vider la poubelle, jouer au football, and faire des courses.

Demain, le président va au sommet du G7 — Tomorrow, the president goes to / is going to the G7 summit — has present va and a destination, without an infinitive. It has future meaning, but is not the grammatical futur proche construction. Likewise Elle va au cinéma demain uses a present form for a planned future action. Compare Elle va regarder un film demain, which uses aller + infinitive. Read the whole construction: movement followed by an infinitive can also express purpose, as in going somewhere to fetch something.

The futur simple also occurs in ordinary speech and writing, not only formal or political speeches: Demain, je commanderai une pizza. Choosing between future forms depends on the speaker’s viewpoint and context, not a fixed certainty percentage or a rule equating one form with casual speech and the other with seriousness.

Order takeout and arrange collection

Use Je vais prendre + noun to say what you will have/order. Here prendre is the infinitive after vais; the food or drink follows it.

Model English
Je vais prendre une commande à emporter, s’il vous plaît. I’m going to place a takeout order, please.
Je vais prendre une pizza quatre fromages. I’m going to have a four-cheese pizza.
Je vais prendre des pâtes au saumon. I’m going to have the salmon pasta.
Mon collègue, il va prendre des pâtes au saumon. My colleague is going to have the salmon pasta.
Je vais commander une boisson. I’m going to order a drink.
Nous allons venir chercher la commande à treize heures. We’ll come to pick up the order at 1 p.m.

Repeating the topic with il in Mon collègue, il… is conversational; a neutral written form can simply use Mon collègue va…. Venir chercher means come and get / pick up; aller chercher means go and get, with a different direction relative to the speaker or reference place. Servir le plat means serve the dish; prendre un verre is the idiom have a drink.

For visiting people, rendre visite à is the standard learner model; visiter is the usual choice for places such as cities or museums, although contextual and regional uses with people exist. Football / foot means soccer; football américain means American football. Faire la lessive / les lessives means do the laundry. Faire des courses can mean go shopping or run errands; groceries are one common context.

Greetings, game language and pronunciation practice

Je suis ravi de vous voir — I’m delighted to see you — uses ravi for a masculine speaker, ravie for a feminine speaker; plural subjects require plural agreement. Tout le monde est en forme ? asks whether everyone is feeling well. Rebonjour ! Ça va tout le monde ? Ça s’est bien passé ? means hello again, is everyone doing well, did it go well? For a completed experience, use Ça s’est bien passé ? rather than C’était bien passé ?. Avez-vous des questions ? is an inversion question (“Do you have any questions?”); Ça va tout le monde ? is informal. Close with À tout à l’heure — See you shortly/later.

Serpents et échelles is snakes and ladders; un joueur is a player. For conjugation practice, choose Catégorie (category) and three or four players (trois / quatre joueurs), roll with Lancer le dé, then change a present-tense sentence to the imparfait. A wrong choice can be marked with une croix (an X) and the correct form. Compare the complete answer with a conjugation reference before moving to the next turn. C’est le tour du joueur suivant means it’s the next player’s turn; the general pattern is C’est le tour de + person/role. Qui a gagné ? means who won? A conjugueur is a conjugation tool; conjugué means conjugated. Use a dictionary such as Reverso to check meaning and a conjugation reference to check forms.

For pronunciation practice, use the IPA (English abbreviation) or API (alphabet phonétique international, French abbreviation), compare mouth positions and discriminate sounds, then read short paragraphs aloud for rhythm and intonation. 20. True or false: ordinary c before o or a gives /s/. Explain the role of ç.

False: ordinary c before a/o/u normally gives /k/. Ç keeps /s/ before those vowels. Ordinary c before e/i/y normally gives /s/; c + r is not a soft-c rule.

21. In which two imparfait forms of manger is the extra e omitted? Contrast the present nous form.

Nous mangions and vous mangiez omit the extra e before i. The other imparfait forms have mangea-. Present nous mangeons retains e before o.

22. Conjugate aller in the present tense (all forms).

je vais, tu vas, il va, elle va, on va, nous allons, vous allez, ils vont, elles vont.

23. Identify the tense in En général, j’avais de bonnes notes and explain the viewpoint.

Imparfait: avais describes a habitual/general past situation. En général helps the interpretation but does not mechanically require that tense.

24. Which expression points to future time: hier, demain or toujours? Does it alone prove futur proche?

Demain means tomorrow. It can accompany a present form, futur proche or futur simple; inspect the verb construction.

25. Is Demain, le président va au sommet du G7 grammatically futur proche or present? Explain.

Present: va is present aller followed by a destination, not an infinitive. Demain gives future meaning without changing the verb’s grammatical form.
