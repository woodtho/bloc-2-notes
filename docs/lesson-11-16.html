Events and background in the past

The passé composé presents an event as a completed whole, a change or a step in a narrative: it answers Qu’est-ce qui s’est passé ? — What happened? It can translate “I played”, “I have played” and emphatic “I did play”; context supplies distinctions that English sometimes expresses with different forms. Il a mangé toute la viande can mean he ate/has eaten/did eat all the meat.

The imparfait describes an ongoing situation, repeated habit, background, state, feeling, thought or supporting detail: “I was playing”, “I used to play”, or sometimes “I played”. This is a difference of viewpoint, not a rule that one tense is only for short actions and the other only for long actions. Compare Il pleuvait quand je suis sorti — It was raining when I went out; and Quand j’étais petit, je jouais souvent dehors — When I was little, I often played outside. Recognizing the forms in reading/listening is a useful first step before producing them fluently.

Form the passé composé and learn participles

Use present-tense avoir/être + past participle. Conjugate the auxiliary, not an infinitive: j’ai regardé le film (I watched the film), not je avoir regarder le film.

Regular -er → -é, as in jouer → joué; regular second-group -ir → -i, as in finir → fini, grandir → grandi, choisir → choisi. Many -re verbs have -u, such as attendre → attendu, descendre → descendu, but this is not an exceptionless rule. The -er infinitive and its -é participle usually sound alike while their spellings and grammatical roles differ.

Learn avoir → eu securely and practise a manageable number of irregulars at a time, such as two per day. Être itself takes avoir as a main verb: elle a été ministre, elles ont été contentes; the adjective contentes agrees even though the participle été does not change.

Choose the auxiliary by the verb and its construction

Most verbs take avoir. A limited set takes être in particular intransitive meanings: devenir, revenir, mourir, rentrer, sortir, venir, arriver, naître, descendre, entrer, rester, tomber, retourner, aller, monter, partir, and relevant compounds such as parvenir. Mnemonics include Dr & Mrs Vandertramp, a “house of verbs” diagram and opposite pairs naître/mourir, venir/aller, entrer/sortir, arriver/partir. Movement alone is not a test: marcher and voyager take avoir. Nor does every -venir ending guarantee être: contrevenir takes avoir, while convenir depends on meaning/usage.

Some movement verbs — sortir, rentrer, passer, descendre, monter, retourner — take avoir when used transitively, with a direct object. French is commonly subject–verb–object in a neutral declarative sentence. Ask quoi ? (what?) after the verb: an answer attached without à, de, sur, dans, etc. can identify the direct object. A location answers où ? (where?) and is not that object. For example, Nous sommes restés à la maison (We stayed at home) keeps être because à la maison is a location.

Intransitive/location construction: être Direct-object construction: avoir
Il est sorti dans la rue. (He went out into the street) Il a sorti le chien / J’ai sorti les poubelles. (He took out the dog) (I took out the garbage cans)
Nous sommes rentrés. Nous avons rentré la voiture.
Ils sont passés devant la banque. Ils ont passé de bonnes vacances.
Il est monté. Nous avons monté l’escalier / Il a monté les valises. (We went up the stairs)
Je suis entré dans la salle. (I entered the room) J’ai entré les données.

Entrer dans is a location construction, not proof that entrer can never be transitive. Il est sorti le chien and nous avons resté à la maison choose the wrong auxiliary for their intended constructions. Rentrer by itself is not a reflexive verb: a pronominal construction has a matching pronoun.

Participle agreement: être, avoir and pronominal verbs

With ordinary être verbs, agree with the subject: masculine singular has the base form, feminine singular adds -e, masculine/mixed plural -s, feminine plural -es. Thus allé / allée / allés / allées. Conventional mixed-group agreement uses masculine plural; an all-female group uses feminine plural. Nous/vous therefore require the form appropriate to the people addressed/referred to, including polite singular vous. Agreement follows the subject, not hier soir, à la maison or another complement.

Subject Example agreement
il arrivé, devenu
elle allée, tombée, restée
a masculine/mixed group rentrés, descendus, nés, morts, restés
a feminine group venues, allées

In papa est devenu un héros, papa is masculine singular; the final written s belongs to singular héros, not to a plural subject. Do not omit the required e in Elle est allée au parc (She went to the park).

With avoir, the participle normally does not agree with the subject: Deux amis ont regardé le match. A preceding direct object can require agreement: On les a rencontrés (We met them), or les lettres que j’ai écrites. A following direct object does not trigger subject agreement: elle a sorti la poubelle.

Pronominal/reflexive verbs use être, often in hygiene/routine verbs se lever, se brosser les dents, se réveiller, se reposer, se souvenir. Their agreement needs its own analysis: elle s’est levée / elles se sont lavées, but elle s’est brossé les dents / elles se sont lavé les mains have a following direct object, and elles se sont parlé has an indirect reflexive object. Do not infer automatic subject agreement solely from the auxiliary être. Models include Nous nous sommes levés (We got up), Vous vous êtes bien reposé(e)(s) (You rested well), and Quebec’s motto Je me souviens.

Form the imparfait

Take the present nous form, remove -ons, and add -ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient. Être is exceptional: nous sommes supplies no -ons base, so use ét-. Other stems include parl-, descend-, fais-, pren-, part-; prenai- incorrectly includes part of an ending.

Subject jouer finir attendre aller être
je jouais finissais attendais allais étais
tu jouais finissais attendais allais étais
il/elle/on jouait finissait attendait allait était
nous jouions finissions attendions allions étions
vous jouiez finissiez attendiez alliez étiez
ils/elles jouaient finissaient attendaient allaient étaient

Use j’ before the vowel-initial forms. Do not identify a tense from a letter sequence alone: present nous étudions and imperfect nous étudiions show why the complete form matters; some forms of other verbs can coincide. Il se réveillait tard (He used to wake up late) needs se and the word tard, not tare. Elle partait à six heures (She was leaving / used to leave at six o’clock) describes a past background/habit; il se lavait (He was washing himself) and il finissait de manger (He was finishing eating) are also imperfect examples.

Pronunciation, vocabulary and reusable patterns

Ç gives /s/ before a/o/u, as in français, garçon, rather than hard /k/. Final -é commonly has close /e/; imperfect -ais/-ait/-aient commonly has open /ɛ/, with regional variation and some mergers. Practise travaillé / travaillait, parlé / parlait and listen for the auxiliary/context too. A mirror can help with the more open vowel; English mouth-shape comparisons are only rough cues. The written -ent within imperfect -aient is not a separate sound, and it is not -ont.

Added e/s in many participles are silent: allé/allée/allés, arrivés, descendus. Héros has h aspiré: it has no /h/ sound but blocks liaison/elision (le héros, no /z/ in les héros). Connect subject, auxiliary and participle naturally: Quelqu’un est entré; (Someone came in) Ils ont été contents; Vous avez vu ? Pauses for emphasis/hesitation are still possible.

Un ministre is a person; un ministère is a ministry/department, not that person. Rendre visite à is the usual learner model for visiting people; visiter is the ordinary model for places, museums and exhibitions, with some contextual/regional uses for people. Avoir de la chance means be lucky. Le balcon, une poubelle, faire les courses / du shopping, participer, préparer, and résoudre un problème are related vocabulary. A plural adjective before its noun normally changes indefinite des → de: de bons balcons; set expressions and other determiner constructions can behave differently.

Comment est-ce que tu as fait pour + infinitive ? asks how someone managed to do something. Désolé(e), je suis resté(e) plus longtemps que prévu means sorry, I stayed longer than expected. Tu as abusé de l’insecticide means you overused the insecticide. For “See you Wednesday”, say À mercredi / Je vais vous voir mercredi, without à before the weekday in that complete statement. Use the French tense names to avoid confusing passé composé with English past perfect/pluperfect.

4. What is the difference between visiter and rendre visite?

visiter = places/sites; rendre visite (à) = people

present of avoir or être + past participle

13. Choose the auxiliary: Il sorti dans la rue. / Il sorti le chien.

14. Conjugate finir and être in the imparfait (all persons).

finir: je finissais, tu finissais, il/elle/on finissait, nous finissions, vous finissiez, ils/elles finissaient — être: j'étais, tu étais, il/elle/on était, nous étions, vous étiez, ils/elles étaient

15. Give the imparfait stem of: parler, faire, prendre, aller.

parl-, fais-, pren-, all-. The source incorrectly included the imperfect ending in prenai-.

16. Why does "papa est devenu un héros" use singular "devenu"?

"papa" is masculine singular; the s in "héros" is part of the word, not a plural marker

e.g., Je suis sorti avec mes amis.

25. Give the usual learner model for visiting a person: Il a visité son père.

26. Correct: Nous avons resté à la maison.

27. Correct: Elle est sorti la poubelle.

28. A student says rentré is a reflexive verb. Is this correct? Why or why not?

Rentrer by itself is not pronominal. A pronominal construction has a matching pronoun, such as se lever.
