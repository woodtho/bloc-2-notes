Days and months in complete sentences

Ask Quel jour sommes-nous aujourd’hui ?, Quel jour étions-nous hier ?, and Quel jour serons-nous demain ?. The source’s plural quels jours is unnecessary when asking about one day. Answers include Aujourd’hui, c’est lundi (Today is Monday), Nous sommes lundi, Hier, nous étions dimanche (It was Sunday), Demain, nous serons mardi / Ce sera mardi / On sera mardi. Three days before Monday is Friday: Nous étions vendredi il y a trois jours (Three days ago it was Friday), not Saturday.

Do not put à after étions for a weekday: Nous étions dimanche, not Nous étions à dimanche. Day and month names are normally lowercase except at the start of a sentence or where title conventions require a capital. Thus en septembre, not a capitalized month in the middle of an ordinary sentence.

For a month, use en even before a vowel: Nous sommes en octobre (It is October) / On est en octobre. Ask Quel mois sommes-nous ? / On est en quel mois ?. Spell all twelve: janvier, février, mars, avril, mai, juin, juillet, août, septembre, octobre, novembre, décembre.

The first letters correspond to the English month names, but watch the next letters in avril/April and août/August. The endings -bre in septembre, octobre, novembre, décembre differ from English -ber. Write mai, with i, not mais, which means but.

For numerical dates use le + day + month: le 15 octobre. The first of a month uses premier (le premier mai); other days use cardinal numbers. Quelle est la date de l’événement ? — L’événement a lieu le 15 octobre asks and answers an event date.

Birthdays, tasks and year expressions

Ask Quand est ton/votre anniversaire ?, C’est quand ton anniversaire ?, or in Québec Quand est ta fête ?. Quelle est la date de ton anniversaire ? is a clear date question; the source’s Quel est ton anniversaire ? is less natural for this purpose. Generic answers can be L’anniversaire est en juin / en mars / en septembre / en mai.

Task questions include En quel mois fait-on la déclaration d’impôts ? (In which month do we do the tax return?), En quel mois fait-on un dépôt au REER ?, En quel mois est le jour de l’an ?, and En quel mois est votre anniversaire ?. Answer C’est en + month. These are language structures, not guidance on actual filing or contribution deadlines.

The source’s administrative exercise associated avril with the federal fiscal-year start and typical tax-return discussion, mars with a year-end/performance-review context, mars/avril or sometimes novembre with a budget discussion, novembre with le jour du souvenir, octobre with Halloween, and mai with Victoria Day. A suggested 5 juillet annual-report answer was unresolved. Keep these as source exercise examples; actual administrative schedules vary and were not verified in the lesson.

For a year, en 1967 / en 2000 / en 2026 is the basic construction. Practise mille neuf cent soixante-sept, deux mille, deux mille vingt-six, and mille neuf cent quatre-vingt-dix. The full mille… reading is dependable. Contrary to the source’s blanket “Anglicism” label, dix-neuf cent soixante-sept is an established way to read certain years, not automatically incorrect French. A particular course may prefer one model, but that preference is not a universal rule.

Ask En quelle année es-tu né/ née ? / Tu es né/ née en quelle année ?. In these examples, Je suis né en deux mille dix (I was born in 2010) and Je suis née en deux mille dix differ in participle agreement. For ordinary être birth constructions, agreement follows the subject’s gender and number. Practise J’ai commencé un nouvel emploi (I started a new job) / J’ai obtenu un poste / J’ai commencé à travailler pour un programme statistique. Commencé needs the accent; commence is another form. Commencer à travailler needs à before the infinitive, whereas travailler can take several complements, not always à.

Proper organization names follow their official capitalization: Statistique Canada is the French name, Statistics Canada the English name. Le programme de l’indice des prix à la consommation supplies additional administrative vocabulary.

For feminine/masculine/plural birth subjects, the forms include il est né, elle est née, ils sont nés, elles sont nées. 1986 reads mille neuf cent quatre-vingt-six.

Seasons and their different constructions

Ask En quelle saison sommes-nous ? / C’est quelle saison ?. Use C’est l’automne (It’s autumn) / l’été / l’hiver to identify the season, or On est en automne / en été / en hiver to locate the present in it. These communicate similar information but have different grammatical structures. Le printemps takes au: Les fleurs poussent au printemps (The flowers grow in spring).

Ask Quelle est votre saison préférée ?. A model is Ma saison préférée, c’est l’hiver parce que j’aime faire de la planche à neige (My favourite season is winter because I like to snowboard); another is Je préfère le printemps parce que les fleurs poussent. Comment dit-on le printemps en anglais ? asks for the English term, spring; English autumn/fall both correspond to automne.

Greeting versus parting, with every form

Purpose Forms
greet during the day bonjour
greet in the evening bonsoir
informal greeting salut
greet on the telephone; regional casual greeting allô
wish someone a good day/evening bonne journée / bonne soirée
wish a good period before/after noon bon avant-midi / bon après-midi; feminine après-midi also occurs
wish a good lunch in Canadian usage bon dîner (Enjoy your lunch (Quebec))
part until a known time à demain / à mercredi / à bientôt / à tout à l’heure / à tout de suite (See you Wednesday)
bedtime/nighttime parting bonne nuit (Good night (I’m going to bed))

The short/long contrast is useful: le jour → la journée, le soir → la soirée, bonjour/bonsoir for greeting versus bonne journée/bonne soirée for a wish on parting. Une nuitée does exist, particularly for an overnight stay, but it is not the normal farewell word; say bonne nuit. Après-midi can be masculine or feminine; masculine is often preferred in formal references. Avant-midi is especially Canadian and its gender varies by reference and use.

Bonne nuit is generally for bedtime or a nighttime farewell, not the normal greeting on entering an evening gathering; use bonsoir then. Bon matin is heard in Québec and other Canadian contexts, with usage advice varying; bonjour is the broadly useful default. Dîner often means lunch in Canada but the evening meal in France. Allô is not exclusive to Québec: it is also a common telephone greeting elsewhere.

The time-noun pairs include jour/journée, matin/matinée, soir/soirée. Long forms emphasize a period/experience, as in une bonne journée / une longue soirée; they can still take numbers, as in trois belles années.

Pronunciation and accent marks

Août can be pronounced with or without final /t/, according to region and speaker; neither variant should be reduced to a universal “correct in Canada, wrong elsewhere” rule. Mars normally has a pronounced final /s/ and also an /r/; the source’s approximate “mass” spelling must not imply that the r disappears. Mai commonly sounds like mets. Practise janvier, février, juin, juillet. The first consonant of juillet is French /ʒ/, not exactly the English /dʒ/ in “juice.”

French rhythm gives prominence to the end of a group; it is not a rule to artificially stress every month’s last syllable. En has a nasal vowel. Printemps ends with a nasal vowel and normally silent final s; été has the /e/ vowels rather than the source’s “liti” guess; hiver has a mute h and a pronounced final r. Ou (or) and où (where) are normally pronounced the same: the written accent distinguishes their meaning, not a different “u” sound.

Accents belong to correct spelling. Use a keyboard layout or character entry method that lets you write é, è, ê, à, ç, etc. Bon après-midi and bonne après-midi may sound very similar in connected speech, though gender agreement remains visible in writing.

Questions

1. Translate “the day after tomorrow”.

après-demain

2. Translate “the day before yesterday”.

avant-hier

3. Give the French for January, July and August.

janvier; juillet; août

4. Complete Nous sommes ___ octobre.

en

5. Which two month names do not share their first two letters with English?

avril (April); août (August)

6. Translate spring, summer, autumn and winter.

le printemps; l’été; l’automne; l’hiver

7. Translate “snowboarding” using Canadian French.

la planche à neige

8. What is the official French name of Statistics Canada?

Statistique Canada.

9. Translate le jour du Souvenir.

Remembrance Day

10. What does bon dîner wish someone in Quebec usage?

An enjoyable lunch. Dîner commonly denotes the midday meal there.

11. Which Canadian greeting corresponds to good morning, and what is the neutral alternative?

Bon matin is heard in Canadian French and is sometimes criticized as an English calque; bonjour is the widely accepted neutral greeting.

12. Complete Hier, nous ___ dimanche.

étions

13. Give two future ways to say “Tomorrow will be Tuesday”.

Demain, on sera mardi / ce sera mardi / nous serons mardi.

14. True or false: French month names always begin with capitals.

False: normally lowercase, except at the start of a sentence or where another capitalization convention applies.

15. Read 1967 in full French number words.

mille neuf cent soixante-sept

16. Read 2026 and 2005.

deux mille vingt-six; deux mille cinq

17. Which preposition means “in” before a year?

en

18. Complete Je suis ___ en deux mille dix for a feminine subject.

née

19. Complete On est ___ hiver; les fleurs poussent ___ printemps.

en; au

20. Give the farewell wishes corresponding to jour and soir.

Bonne journée; bonne soirée

21. Is après-midi masculine, feminine or both?

Both genders are accepted.

22. Say that today is Monday.

24. Use 2010 as a birth year in a sentence.

25. Give a favourite season and a reason.

26. Say “See you Wednesday”.

27. Correct Nous étions à samedi.

Nous étions samedi.

28. Is Je suis né en dix-neuf cent quatre-vingt-dix inherently incorrect? Give the full-number alternative.

No. Dix-neuf cent is an established year reading. The full alternative is Je suis né en mille neuf cent quatre-vingt-dix. A particular exercise may ask for that form.

29. Correct en printemps.

au printemps

30. Greet a colleague arriving at 9 a.m. instead of saying Bonne nuit.

Bonjour; allô/salut in suitable informal contexts. Bonne nuit is a farewell before sleep.
