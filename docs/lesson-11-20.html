Review the futur proche

The futur proche uses present aller + an infinitive: je vais, tu vas, il/elle/on va, nous allons, vous allez, ils/elles vont + infinitive. Conjugate only aller; keep the following verb in the infinitive. It commonly expresses plans, intentions or a future connected with the present; it is not restricted to the next few minutes.

To ask about tomorrow, use Qu’est-ce que tu vas faire demain ? — What are you going to do tomorrow? These are generic models:

French English
Je vais aller en ville pour un tournoi de karaté. I’m going to go into town for a karate tournament.
Je vais aller voir mon fils jouer au hockey. I’m going to go watch my son play hockey.
Je vais faire l’épicerie. I’m going to do grocery shopping.
Je vais conduire ma fille et ses amis au cinéma. I’m going to drive my daughter and her friends to the cinema.
Je vais changer les pneus. I’m going to change the tires.
Je vais jouer au badminton avec mes amis. I’m going to play badminton with my friends.
Ma famille va faire du bénévolat. My family is going to do volunteer work.

Je vais aller en ville remains futur proche even though aller appears twice: the first is conjugated and the second is an infinitive. Faire l’épicerie is common Canadian/Quebec French for grocery shopping; le supermarché is a supermarket and le marché a market.

Form the futur simple

The futur simple is a one-word conjugated tense, usually translated with English will. It has no separate auxiliary corresponding to English will: use future stem + ending. The future stem ends in r, which is also used in the conditional.

Subject Ending ranger: to tidy accomplir: to accomplish
je -ai je rangerai j’accomplirai
tu -as tu rangeras tu accompliras
il/elle/on -a il/elle/on rangera il/elle/on accomplira
nous -ons nous rangerons nous accomplirons
vous -ez vous rangerez vous accomplirez
ils/elles -ont ils rangeront / elles rangeront ils/elles accompliront

All verbs use -ai, -as, -a, -ons, -ez, -ont, including irregular verbs. Remember the present of avoir: j’ai, tu as, il/elle/on a, nous avons, vous avez, ils/elles ont. The endings resemble those forms, but -ons/-ez are not the complete words avons/avez.

For regular -er and -ir verbs, use the full infinitive as the stem: ranger-, accomplir-. Some verbs irregular in the present also follow that future pattern: ouvrir → j’ouvrirai, partir → partira. Le prochain train partira demain matin — The next train will leave tomorrow morning. Other forms to recognize are vous réussirez (you will succeed) and ils oublieront (they will forget).

For verbs following the -re pattern, remove only the final silent e, then add the ending:

Infinitive Future form English
rendre je rendrai / nous rendrons I will give back / we will give back
vendre je vendrai I will sell
pendre je pendrai I will hang
prendre je prendrai / ils prendront I will take / they will take
craindre je craindrai I will fear
éteindre j’éteindrai I will turn off
joindre je joindrai I will join / reach
battre je battrai I will beat

Do not blend a present form into the future: an invented form such as je vends-jarai is incorrect. The rule does not remove irregular future stems: check the irregular list below.

Learn one future stem for all subjects. Unlike many present-tense patterns, a spelling change in the future also applies to nous/vous. Add the appropriate ending to that same stem. Je rangerai ma chambre — I will tidy my room; Nous rangerons la maison — We will tidy the house.

Spelling changes and accepted alternatives

Keep the infinitive spelling in -cer/-ger future forms: lancer → je lancerai (I will throw/launch), manger → je mangerai (I will eat). The following e already gives the relevant soft consonant, so do not import present lançons/mangeons spellings into these future stems.

Pattern Future examples Detail
-oyer / -uyer nettoyer → je nettoierai; essuyer → j’essuierai y becomes i; envoyer has the separate irregular stem enverr-.
-ayer essayer → j’essaierai / j’essayerai Both spellings are accepted.
appeler j’appellerai, tu appelleras, il/elle/on appellera, nous appellerons, vous appellerez, ils/elles appelleront Double l throughout the future paradigm.
jeter je jetterai Double t; keep the same stem for every subject.
plain e before consonant + er lever → je lèverai; amener → j’amènerai; peser → je pèserai; acheter → j’achèterai; achever → j’achèverai The relevant e becomes è.
é before consonant + er, traditional spelling céder → je céderai; espérer → j’espérerai; répéter → je répéterai; célébrer → je célébrerai These are the traditional forms used in the lesson.
é before consonant + er, rectified spelling je cèderai; j’espèrerai; je répèterai; je célèbrerai The 1990 spelling rectifications also permit è in these future forms.

The lesson’s memory aid is plain e takes è; an existing é stays in traditional future spelling. Keep the qualification about spelling conventions: it is not a rule that every é spelling must remain unchanged. Nor does every -eler/-eter verb double its consonant; acheter uses è, whereas appeler/jeter keep their doubled consonants. Consult un conjugueur (a conjugation tool) for an unfamiliar verb.

Accueillir is an -ir exception: accueiller- → j’accueillerai — I will welcome. Retain the distinction from ouvrir → j’ouvrirai. Practise j’accueille / j’accueillerai as complete forms, rather than guessing a future from the last two infinitive letters.

Avoid present-tense interference: nous appelons → nous appellerons when future meaning is intended; vous accomplissez → vous accomplirez, not vous accomplez. J’apparais, tu apparais, ils apparaissent belong to apparaître (to appear), not appeler (to call): use j’appellerai, tu appelleras, ils appelleront for the future of calling.

Irregular future stems

The endings remain the same; memorize the stem. This list also prepares you for the conditionnel présent, which uses the same future stems with different endings.

Infinitive Future stem Model English
aller ir- j’irai I will go
courir courr- je courrai I will run
devoir devr- je devrai I will have to
envoyer enverr- j’enverrai I will send
faire fer- je ferai I will do / make
falloir faudr- il faudra It will be necessary
mourir mourr- je mourrai I will die
obtenir obtiendr- j’obtiendrai I will obtain
pleuvoir pleuvr- il pleuvra It will rain
pouvoir pourr- je pourrai I will be able to
recevoir recevr- je recevrai / vous recevrez I / you will receive
savoir saur- je saurai / elle saura I / she will know
tenir tiendr- je tiendrai / il tiendra I / he will hold
venir viendr- je viendrai I will come
voir verr- je verrai / tu verras I / you will see
vouloir voudr- je voudrai / ils voudront I / they will want

avoir aur- elle aura / elles auront She / they will have
conquérir conquerr- il conquerra He will conquer

Falloir is impersonal. Weather pleuvoir normally uses il; figurative uses need not be impersonal. Watch written double r in courrai, mourrai, pourrai, verrai, conquerra and enverrai. Obtenir follows the tenir family.

Je voudrai means I will want; je voudrais is conditional, often I would like. Thus Je voudrai te voir demain means I will want to see you tomorrow. Je voudrais te voir demain is a valid request with a different meaning. Likewise je rangerai / je rangerais share a stem but have different endings. The conditional is only a preview here.

Use the future in context

Common markers are bientôt (soon), ce soir (tonight), demain (tomorrow) and un jour (one day). They indicate time; inspect the verb to identify its tense. Both future constructions can refer to plans or predictions; do not assign a fixed time distance to either.

French English / use
Nous serons bientôt là. We will be there soon.
Un jour, je visiterai l’Australie. One day I will visit Australia; future intention or hope.
Je pense qu’il arrivera en retard. I think he will arrive late; a prediction.
Je serai de retour dans dix-huit jours exactement. I will be back in exactly eighteen days.
Vous reprendrez bien un peu de café. You’ll have a little more coffee; a polite offer, often understood as “Won’t you have a little more coffee?”
Ils iront au Mexique pour Noël. They will go to Mexico for Christmas.
Vous recevrez une invitation dans les prochains jours. You will receive an invitation in the coming days.
Ils iront se promener dans le bois. They will go for a walk in the woods.
J’espère que tu seras à l’heure. I hope you will be on time.
Il ne mourra pas. He will not die.
Il conquerra. He will conquer.

After a conjunction of time

For a future event after quand, lorsque/lorsqu’ (when), dès que/dès qu’ (as soon as), or après que/après qu’ (after), French normally uses a future tense where English often uses the present. A subordinate clause is the part introduced by the linking expression; you can apply the pattern without memorizing the terminology.

French English
Appelle-moi quand tu recevras l’invitation. Call me when you get the invitation.
Vois avec elle dès qu’elle sera de retour. Check with her as soon as she is back.
La prochaine fois qu’on voyagera, nous n’oublierons pas notre valise. Next time we travel, we won’t forget our suitcase.
Quand tu arriveras, nous commencerons. When you arrive, we will begin.

The first two main clauses are imperatives, not future forms. Both clauses can be future, as in the last row, but they do not have to be. For an intended single future event, use Appelle-moi quand tu arriveras, rather than mechanically copying English when you arrive as quand tu arrives. A present form remains possible for a habit or a different context. These conjunctions do not automatically force future time in every sentence.

After après que, a completed future action commonly uses the futur antérieur: Après que tu auras terminé, nous partirons — After you have finished, we will leave. That is an indicative future construction, not a rule to put every clause in futur simple.

Pronunciation, listening and negation

Pronounce the future stem’s r, especially if your English accent drops r after vowels. Tu demanderas son adresse à ses parents — You will ask his/her parents for the address. The r in demanderas must remain audible; tu demandea loses part of the form. French commonly uses a back-of-mouth/throat r, though its realization varies. It helps distinguish forms, but is not unique to the future: the conditional also contains it. Practise short, comfortable repetitions without forcing the tongue or throat.

É (accent aigu) generally represents closed /e/; è (accent grave) represents open /ɛ/. These are the vowel qualities used to compare future -ai and conditional -ais. A useful Quebec model is je rangerai with final /e/, roughly “rangeré,” versus je rangerais with /ɛ/, roughly “rangerè.” Regional varieties can merge or realize these vowels differently; spelling, subject and context still matter. IPA is a transcription system, not a rule prescribing one open vowel for all future -ai forms.

In j’appelle / j’appellerai, the vowel before ll is open /ɛ/; it is separate from the final future vowel. Link nous appellerons, vous appellerez, ils appelleront smoothly, with /z/ liaison before the vowel. Treat subject and verb as a connected spoken group rather than stopping between them; use manageable phrase lengths and natural breathing.

Distinguish elle saura (she will know) from elle sera (she will be). For “She will know a lot about history,” use Elle saura beaucoup de choses sur l’histoire, not Elle sera beaucoup de choses sur l’histoire. Also compare elle aura (she will have) with elles auront (they will have): the final vowels and liaison help identify number. Careful listening to -ai/-ais and the whole verb matters more than a single letter cue.

In informal spoken French, ne is often omitted while pas remains. Written Elles n’auront pas le temps — They won’t have time — may be heard as Elles auront pas le temps, with /z/ linking elles to auront. Retain ne/n’ in ordinary formal writing. Oulala, ce pull est bien sale. Une bonne lessive ne sera pas inutile — Oh dear, this sweater is really dirty. A good wash won’t be useless — combines future sera and negation; ne sera pas inutile suggests the wash will be useful.

Vocabulary distinctions and instructions

Possessive determiners reflect the noun possessed, not the owner’s gender: ma fille et ses amis, not ma fille et her amis. Ses amis means his/her friends; son ami means his/her male friend. English son (male child) is fils, as in mon fils, not mon son. A girl/daughter is une fille.

Use un tournoi de karaté / un tournoi de hockey for a sports tournament. Une tournée means a tour or round, such as a performer’s tour. For “I’m going to go to a hockey tournament,” write Je vais aller à un tournoi de hockey, not à une tournée de hockey. Keep le pendule (pendulum) distinct from la pantoufle (slipper).

Je vous rappelle que + clause means I remind you that… A date reminder is Je vous rappelle que le rendez-vous est le lundi 30 novembre — I remind you that the appointment is on Monday, November 30. The conversational … le rendez-vous, c’est le lundi 30 novembre uses a detached topic plus c’est.

Il faut taper dans les espaces vides la conjugaison du verbe que vous voyez entre parenthèses — Type the conjugated form of the verb shown in parentheses into the blank spaces. Vérifier les réponses means check the answers. Le clavardage is Quebec French for online chat; la transcription can mean a transcript or live captions. Serpents et échelles is snakes and ladders; une équipe de deux / trois is a team of two / three. For recognition practice, identify the infinitive, subject, stem and ending, then check the negative markers and meaning. Games and fill-in-the-blank exercises can consolidate those same skills.

18. What happens to verbs following the -re future pattern before adding future endings?

19. How do plain-e verbs such as acheter and é verbs such as céder form their future spellings?

Plain e becomes è: j'achèterai. Traditional é spelling stays in je céderai; rectified je cèderai is also accepted. The same alternatives apply to espérer, répéter and célébrer.
