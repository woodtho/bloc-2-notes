Quantity questions

Use combien for “how much/how many”, combien de + noun for a quantity and combien coûte…? for a price. A full question normally needs a verb: Combien de personnes y a-t-il ? — How many people are there? The construction is il y a, with il retained in an ordinary “there are” answer. Do not add a redundant nombre in Combien de nombre de personnes ?

  • De combien de copies as-tu besoin ? — How many copies do you need? De combien de tables avez-vous besoin ? — De deux tables. Both initial de and de before the counted noun have a role: avoir besoin de requires its preposition. The source’s versions without initial de have been corrected.
  • Combien coûte cet agenda ? — How much does this planner cost? Quel est le prix de ces imprimantes ? — What is the price of these printers?
  • Dans combien de provinces parle-t-on français ? — In how many provinces is French spoken?
  • À combien d’associations appartenez-vous ? — How many associations do you belong to? De elides to d’ before associations, while à belongs to appartenir à.
  • Pour combien de personnes préparez-vous le petit déjeuner le matin ? — For how many people do you prepare breakfast in the morning? Here it is préparer pour, not préparer de. For capacity ask Pour combien de personnes la salle de réunion est-elle prévue ?, a more natural model than the source’s Pour combien de personnes la salle de réunion est-elle prévue ?
  • Combien d’années d’expérience avez-vous ? — How many years of experience do you have?
  • À combien de clients parles-tu chaque jour ? — How many clients do you speak to each day? Parler à quelqu’un means speak to someone; parler avec quelqu’un is also possible, with a slightly different construction.

Learn verb-preposition pairings and consult a reference when needed; the exercise’s focus is forming the question, rather than guessing every verb’s preposition.

Combien de personnes ? can be an elliptical conversational follow-up; Combien de personnes y a-t-il ? is the complete question being practised.

Numbers, prices and spelling

Distance practice uses Une ville est à cinq cent quatre-vingt-onze kilomètres d’une autre ville — One city is 591 kilometres from another. Also read 471: quatre cent soixante et onze and 61: soixante et un.

Vingt takes s when multiplied and final in an ordinary number: quatre-vingts, but quatre-vingt-un. Cent similarly takes s when multiplied and not followed by another numeral: six cents, but cent cinquante, six cent cinquante. Mille is invariable: deux mille, trois mille, quatre mille. Million and milliard are nouns: un million, un milliard.

Hyphen examples include soixante-quatorze, quatre-vingt-dix, quatre-vingt-dix-huit. For the complete quantity 1998, say mille neuf cent quatre-vingt-dix-huit. A traditional year reading is dix-neuf cent quatre-vingt-dix-huit; it is an established French form, not simply the English “nineteen ninety-eight”. Traditional and rectified spelling differ in hyphenation: vingt et un / vingt-et-un are the respective systems.

French decimals use a virgule (comma): 3,80. A clear money reading is trois dollars et quatre-vingts cents or trois euros quatre-vingts when that is the currency. Conversational trois quatre-vingt omits the currency; do not assume it means 380. Trois virgule huit zéro explicitly reads the decimal digits. The source’s trois dollars virgule quatre-vingt mixes styles; use the clearer money/decimal patterns. Write amounts such as 3,80 $; dollars is plural in this example. Printer-price practice includes 240: deux cent quarante and 650: six cent cinquante; the export did not specify a currency, so none is inferred.

Ordinals, gender and demonstratives

Practise premier/première, troisième, quinzième, vingt et unième. Première ends in -ère with a grave accent; troisième also has è. Models: La première étape est complétée — The first step is completed; au troisième étage — on the third floor; au quinzième — on the fifteenth.

Deuxième and second/seconde can both mean second. Second often suggests a pair, but “only two” is not an absolute rule; context may not distinguish them. The written c in second/seconde is pronounced /g/.

Une imprimante / des imprimantes is feminine. Cette directrice est exigeante — This director is demanding: the adjective agrees with feminine directrice. Un bureau, un agenda, un fauteuil are masculine; une voiture is feminine. A fauteuil is an armchair, for example beside a sofa.

Ce/cet precede masculine singular nouns: ce fauteuil, cet agenda. Use cet before a vowel sound or mute h. A demonstrative pronoun replaces the noun: celui masculine, celle feminine. Ma voiture est petite. Celle de mon ami est grande — My car is small; my friend’s is large. With bureau, use celui-ci / celui-là — this one/that one. Mon amie uses mon before a feminine vowel-initial noun; in mon ami, the noun itself is masculine.

Une chaise is feminine, while un fauteuil is masculine. Ce bureau est grand. Celui-là est petit — This desk is large; that one is small — illustrates replacing masculine bureau with celui-là.

Phone dialogue

The caller organizes a reunion and wants a hotel reception/conference room. Ask about room size, capacity, tables, refreshments and all costs. Invent the dialogue from the situation rather than reading an answer. Use a role label and a fictional contact placeholder if practising contact questions.

Speaker French English
Caller Bonjour, je voudrais réserver une grande salle de conférence. Hello, I would like to book a large conference room.
Reception Pour combien de personnes ? For how many people?
Caller Il y a cent vingt personnes. There are 120 people.
Caller Quelle est la taille de la salle ? How large is the room?
Reception De combien de tables avez-vous besoin ? How many tables do you need?
Caller D’une dizaine de tables. About ten tables.
Reception Avez-vous besoin de café ou de thé ? Do you need coffee or tea?
Caller Je voudrais du café. Combien coûte le café ? I would like some coffee. How much does the coffee cost?
Reception Les boissons coûtent deux cents dollars et la salle coûte quatre cents dollars. Drinks cost $200 and the room costs $400.
Caller Cela fait six cents dollars au total ? Is that $600 altogether?
Reception Oui. À quel nom dois-je faire la réservation ? Quel numéro de téléphone puis-je noter ? Yes. What name should I put on the booking? What phone number may I note?
Caller Au nom de [nom fictif]. Voici un numéro fictif. Under [fictional name]. Here is a fictional number.

Je voudrais is followed by an infinitive in je voudrais réserver, or by a noun phrase in je voudrais du café. Compris means included; une réservation means a booking.

After booking, ask Est-ce que le café est compris ? — Is coffee included? — and Pouvez-vous confirmer la réservation ? — Can you confirm the booking? Change people, date, start/end times and refreshments to practise the same patterns. Du café is a partitive amount in je voudrais du café.

Alphabet and speaking practice

Practise reciting and spelling aloud with the French alphabet, particularly the names of H, I, J, K, L, M, N, O, P, Q, R through Z. H is ache, J is ji, G is gé: avoid switching English J/G names. Spell the number words above, then answer quantity and reservation questions without reading.
