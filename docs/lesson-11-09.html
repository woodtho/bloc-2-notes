Place an event before, at or after now

Answer quand ? by identifying both the time expression and the verb form. A ligne de temps is a timeline; situer un événement dans le temps means to locate an event in time. A time word helps interpretation but does not mechanically dictate one tense.

Past indicators Present/current indicators Future indicators
hier — yesterday aujourd’hui — today demain — tomorrow
hier soir — last night maintenant — now après-demain — the day after tomorrow
avant-hier — the day before yesterday actuellement — currently bientôt — soon
autrefois — formerly/in the past présentement — currently tantôt — later today in relevant regional use
le mois dernier — last month en ce moment — right now à l’avenir — from now on/in future
la semaine dernière — last week à l’heure actuelle — at present dans l’avenir / dans un proche avenir — in the future / near future
samedi dernier — last Saturday ce matin / cet après-midi / cette semaine — this morning/afternoon/week mardi prochain / la semaine prochaine / le mois prochain — next Tuesday/week/month
à ce moment(-là) — at that moment dans + duration — in a specified time
il y a + duration — a specified time ago

Ce matin and cette semaine can also occur with past or future actions within that period. Après-midi has both genders; cet après-midi is the usual demonstrative phrase. Cette semaine is feminine. No extra article/preposition is needed in demain, aujourd’hui, ce matin when they function directly as time adjuncts.

Initial time phrases set the scene, often followed by a comma: Hier, j’ai travaillé. (Yesterday, I worked) Hier soir, j’ai étudié. (Last night, I studied) Avant-hier, j’étais au bureau. À ce moment-là, j’étais en réunion. (At that moment, I was in a meeting) Final placement is also possible. The source’s “beginning only” and “end only” placement claims are overly restrictive: J’ai terminé la réunion il y a deux heures (I finished the meeting two hours ago) and Il y a deux heures, j’ai terminé la réunion are both grammatical. Similarly, La réunion commence dans trois heures (The meeting starts in three hours) / Dans trois heures, la réunion commence, Je pars en vacances dans trois semaines (I’m leaving on vacation in three weeks), Je termine ce projet dans deux jours. No passé is needed in il y a passé deux jours: say Je l’ai vu il y a deux jours.

The two meanings of il y a

  1. Existence/quantity: Il y a trois cents employés maintenant dans l’édifice — There are three hundred employees in the building now. This is present, not past.
  2. Elapsed time: il y a deux jours / dix ans / vingt ans — two days/ten years/twenty years ago. In this ordinary “ago” construction keep il y a, even with a past main verb: Il y a dix ans, j’ai acheté un appartement dans une rue résidentielle.

Counting commonly uses jours, ans, heures, semaines: dans six ans, il y a deux jours. Longer forms emphasize a period or its character: une belle journée, trois longues années. Numbers do not absolutely prohibit the long forms.

Current time and regional vocabulary

Aujourd’hui, je travaille au bureau (Today, I work at the office) / Je travaille au bureau aujourd’hui differ in emphasis, not correctness. Je suis disponible maintenant. (I am available now) Je travaille actuellement dans un organisme. Nous sommes présentement en période de formation. (We are currently in a training period) En ce moment, je suis très occupé et je travaille. (Right now, I am very busy and I am working) Ce matin, j’ai une réunion. (This morning, I have a meeting) Cet après-midi, je travaille à la maison. Cette semaine, nous avons une formation.

Actuellement is a false friend: it means “currently”, not “actually”. Translate English “actually” as en fait, en réalité, or vraiment where the meaning is “really”. Substituting présentement is a useful check for the “currently” meaning.

À l’heure actuelle is useful in reports or formal/professional writing: À l’heure actuelle, le ministère utilise un nouveau système. (At the present time, the ministry is using a new system) À l’heure actuelle, chaque fonctionnaire dispose d’un ordinateur. (At the present time, each public servant has a computer) It can signal a contrast with the past. It can also occur later in a sentence; “always first” is not a grammar rule. Actuellement/présentement often follow the verb but can open a sentence too.

Présentement is especially ordinary in Canadian French; register/frequency vary elsewhere. Avoid the sweeping source claim that it is essentially confined to archaic/legal French in all of Europe. Tantôt can mean earlier or later today depending on region, context and tense. It is familiar in Quebec/Ontario and also in some European regions; it is not universally forbidden in France. À bientôt / On se voit bientôt (we’ll see each other soon; Farewell without a precise appointment time) are polite farewells with no fixed promise of minutes/hours. Bientôt can precede the sentence: Bientôt, nous partirons, as well as Nous partirons bientôt.

Future expressions and agreement

Demain, j’ai du travail au bureau. (Tomorrow, I have work at the office) Après-demain, j’ai une réunion. A present form can describe an arranged future action. The time expressions can also follow the verb. Dans + duration tells how long until the event, not a physical location. There is no liaison before the consonant in dans deux jours; deux heures has /z/ liaison.

À l’avenir, veuillez vérifier les dates is a recommendation: “From now on, please check the dates.” Dans l’avenir, cette technologie pourrait changer notre façon de travailler (In the future, this technology could change the way we work) looks ahead more generally/speculatively. These are useful tendencies, not encoded percentages of certainty.

Prochain is an adjective: mardi prochain, le mois prochain, la semaine prochaine. Add plural agreement where needed: les semaines prochaines. Masculine prochain has a nasal vowel; feminine prochaine has an audible /n/ and a non-nasal vowel. Mardi prochain, j’ai un rendez-vous. (Next Tuesday, I have an appointment) La semaine prochaine, nous commençons un nouveau projet. (Next week, we start a new project)

The simple future also occurs in promises and softened instructions, not only predictions. Neither its certainty nor its distance from now is fixed by the tense alone.

Recognize every verb form introduced in the source

The indicative mood presents events/situations as facts or realities from the speaker’s viewpoint. The imperative is a separate mood. The source emphasizes active practice of the passé composé and imparfait, with recognition of the more complex tenses.

Form Construction and example English / use
present je mange I eat / I am eating; current action, habit or contextually future plan
imperative mange, mangez, mangeons eat / let’s eat; command or instruction
present progressive expression être en train de + infinitive: je suis en train de manger I am in the process of eating
imperfect je mangeais I used to eat / I was eating; habit, description, background
compound past present avoir/être + past participle: j’ai mangé, elle est arrivée I ate / have eaten; a bounded past event
recent past expression venir de + infinitive: je viens de manger I have just eaten
pluperfect imperfect avoir/être + past participle: j’avais mangé, elle était arrivée I had eaten / she had arrived; earlier than a past reference point
near future present aller + infinitive: je vais manger I am going to eat
simple future je mangerai I will eat; stem plus future ending, with no separate word “will”
future perfect future avoir/être + past participle: j’aurai mangé, elle sera arrivée I will have eaten / she will have arrived

The source omitted être from the pluperfect/future-perfect construction: both auxiliaries are possible, according to the main verb. French has no obligatory progressive tense matching every English “-ing”; je mange already means “I am eating”. Être en train de adds emphasis on the ongoing process.

J’avais déjà mangé quand tu es arrivé — I had already eaten when you arrived. Je mangeais des frites tous les mercredis après l’école — I used to eat fries every Wednesday after school.

Completed events, background and future plans

Hier often accompanies passé composé, but can also accompany imparfait: Hier, il pleuvait quand je suis sorti. Autrefois often introduces an old habit/state: Autrefois, je travaillais à Montréal. (In the past, I used to work in Montreal) Autrefois, j’habitais à Paris. Autrefois, il n’y avait pas de ministère de l’environnement. (In the past, there was no ministry of the environment) Il y a vingt ans, peu de fonctionnaires avaient accès à un ordinateur. It does not impose an absolute ban on a completed-event tense. For a completed purchase use Il y a dix ans, j’ai acheté un appartement, rather than a present j’achète in an ordinary account.

Hier, tout le monde a assisté à la réunion qui a validé le document 102 — Yesterday everyone attended the meeting that approved document 102. The source’s pièce 102 was ambiguous between a room and a document; this model chooses the document meaning rather than treating room approval as a universal phrase.

Futur proche often presents an intention, plan or development connected with now; futur simple often presents a future fact/prediction. Either can express strong or weak certainty. The source’s estimates of 51%, 65%, 95% or 98% are not grammatical probabilities and should not be learned as rules. Nor does a tense establish whether a political promise is honest. Temporal distance alone does not determine the choice.

  • Chaque sous-équipe va présenter le compte rendu de ses discussions. — Each sub-team is going to present its discussion report.
  • Dans l’avenir, la France aura de plus en plus d’importance. — In the future, France will become more important.
  • Dans un proche avenir, tous les fonctionnaires auront un visiophone. — In the near future, all public servants will have a video phone. This dated workbook prediction illustrates future wording, not current technology expectations.
  • Je la verrai encore dans six ans quand je prendrai ma retraite. — I will still see her/it in six years when I retire.
  • Ils arriveront. Nous terminerons. Tu travailleras. Je parlerai. On entendra. — They will arrive; we will finish; you will work; I will speak; one will hear. The future stem’s r is a useful clue, but an /r/ alone does not identify every tense.

Pronunciation and spelling corrections

The French r is commonly uvular, produced toward the back of the mouth; a gentle gargling comparison can help locate it. Pronunciation varies. The source’s claim “pronounce every written r” is false: the final r in many -er infinitives is not pronounced. Avoid forcing or painfully scraping the throat.

É normally represents a close /e/ and è an open /ɛ/: compare the two vowels in élève. A somewhat narrower/more open mouth is a useful cue; exact mouth angles are not phonetic rules. Arrivé has an acute accent, not arrivè. J’ai travaillé / Hier soir, j’ai étudié have /e/ at the end of their past participles.

Distinguish disposer (infinitive, ending /e/), dispose (present, final written e silent) and disposé (past participle, ending /e/). Infinitive and participle can sound the same, but are not the same grammatical form; a disposé needs the auxiliary for compound past. The source’s “silent -er in the present” means use the conjugated form dispose, not that -er is silent in the infinitive.

16. What central question do time indicators answer?

Quand ? — When? They help place an event in the past, present or future.

17. Which tense often accompanies autrefois? Is it compulsory in every sentence?

The imperfect is common for a former habit/state. Autrefois does not impose an absolute prohibition on other past tenses.

18. Must il y a + duration and dans + duration always end the sentence?

No. Both initial and final positions are possible: Il y a deux jours, je suis parti / Je suis parti il y a deux jours; Dans deux jours, je partirai.

19. In the ordinary “ago” construction, must avoir in il y a change to a past tense?

No: J’ai fini il y a deux heures retains il y a.

No: Ce matin, je travaille. Ce/cet/cette already supplies the demonstrative determiner.

21. Must à l’heure actuelle always begin a sentence?

No. Initial position is common in formal reports, but it can appear elsewhere: Le système est disponible à l’heure actuelle.
