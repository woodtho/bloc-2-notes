Event dates, times and agreement

Avoir lieu means take place. Use a pronoun matching the event noun: la réunion / la conférence / la rencontre → elle a lieu; le congrès / le départ → il a lieu. L’arrivée and l’inauguration are feminine. Ask Quand a lieu la conférence ?, À quelle heure a lieu le départ ?, À quelle date et à quelle heure a lieu la conférence ?, or Quelle est la date de l’événement ?

Give the date with le, time with à, and year with en: La réunion a lieu le 26 mars à neuf heures; Nous sommes en 2004 (We are in 2004). Normally present the date before the time in a professional confirmation. A full written date can be le lundi 26 octobre 2026 (weekday, day, month, year); ISO numeric dates may use year-month-day. The details can be presented as separate fields instead. Use no extra à before the numerical date in this event pattern; other prepositions remain possible when their meaning requires them.

For ordinary well-being use Comment allez-vous ? — Je vais bien / Tu vas bien. Je suis bien can mean I feel comfortable/good in a situation; C’est quelqu’un de bien describes a decent person. These expressions are not interchangeable in every context.

Reading years and quatre-vingt(s)

Quatre-vingts takes s when multiplied and final in an ordinary cardinal number. Omit it before another numeral: quatre-vingt-un, quatre-vingt-six, quatre-vingt-huit, quatre-vingt-dix. A rank/number label also omits it: la page quatre-vingt, la chambre quatre-vingt. 1986 = mille neuf cent quatre-vingt-six; 1976 = mille neuf cent soixante-seize; 91 = quatre-vingt-onze. Distinguish soixante-six (66), soixante-dix (70), 1970 and 1976 while listening. Practise readings slowly, then at normal speed. Useful times include dix heures quarante-cinq, quatre heures et demie, and treize heures trente.

Recognize the verb group and subject

The first group has regular -er verbs, except irregular aller. Regular second-group -ir verbs follow finir, choisir, réussir. Other verbs form the third group, including croire, craindre, peindre, joindre, mordre and some -ir verbs. Confiner ends in -er; it is not a second-group -ir verb despite its mistaken listing in the source.

The subjects are je, tu, il, elle, on, nous, vous, ils, elles. Il/elle/on share a conjugation. Separate sujet (subject), radical (stem) and terminaison (ending). For these regular second-group verbs, remove infinitive -ir and add -is, -is, -it, -issons, -issez, -issent. The plural includes -iss-. The s of je finis is a verb ending, not a plural-subject marker; a final s on many subject nouns does mark plural and requires plural agreement.

Regular -ir conjugation reference

Each cell follows je; tu; il/elle/on; nous; vous; ils/elles. Use j’ before a vowel.

Keep é in every form of réussir. Do not substitute compléter for accomplir, grandir (grow) for agrandir (enlarge/zoom), or rayonner (radiate/shine) for réunir (gather). Keep the initial a- in j’agrandis and the full stem in nous agrandissons, not nous agrissons. Use present il finit / ils finissent when the exercise asks for the present, rather than compound-past il a fini / ils ont fini. Les employés rougissent requires -iss-, not rougent. Réagir à / aux données retains its preposition.

Finir and sentence construction

Pattern Meaning Model
finir + object finish something Il finit son livre. (He finishes his book)
finir de + infinitive finish doing something Tu finis de lire ton roman.
finir par + infinitive eventually/end up doing something Tant bien que mal, nous finissons par atteindre le sommet. (Even with difficulty, we end up reaching the summit)
finir à + clock time finish at a time Elle finit à quatre heures et demie.
finir dans + duration finish in a specified time On finit dans trois minutes.

Other finir de examples cover tricoter une écharpe, repasser le linge, atteindre le sommet. Nous finissons d’atteindre le sommet describes completing the reaching process, whereas finissons par atteindre emphasizes eventually succeeding. Nous finissons aujourd’hui is grammatical when the task is understood; adding une tâche meets an exercise that specifically asks for an object. Ask Je finis à quelle heure demain ? or À quelle heure est-ce que…?, and use en quelle saison est-ce que…? for seasons. Je choisis un film pour ce soir (I’m choosing a film for tonight) is another complete present statement. En quelle saison est-ce que les feuilles rougissent au Canada ? (In which season do the leaves turn red in Canada?) links the verb to autumn vocabulary.

Pronunciation: endings, ss, elision and liaison

The final s/t of singular choisis/choisit, finis/finit is normally silent; so is present plural -ent. Ils choisissent ends with /s/, not a nasal /ɑ̃/. Between vowels, single s in choisissons gives /z/, while ss gives /s/. Retain and pronounce the plural -iss- with /s/ in finissent, accomplissons, avertissez, agrandissons, rougissent.

Vowel-initial verbs require je → j’: j’accomplis, j’avertis, j’atterris, j’agrandis. Connect nous/vous/ils/elles + vowel-initial verb with the required /z/ liaison: nous accomplissons, vous accomplissez, ils accomplissent. A gentle throat-vibration check can help distinguish voiced /z/ from /s/; speak as a connected group on one breath without forcing it. A consonant-initial verb such as remplir, réunir, rougir has no such liaison into its initial consonant; retain the subject pronoun even if its final written letters are silent.

Workplace examples involve staff (le personnel), forms, leave requests, project planning and evaluations; clavardage is Canadian French for online chat. Les feuilles rougissent supplies an autumn context. Workbook activity 16, page 20, parts A/B provided the original practice reference; optional time details can be varied without changing the conjugation rule.
