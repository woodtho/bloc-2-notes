Asking the day, capitalization and calendar order

Quel jour sommes-nous aujourd’hui ? is a formal model. Quel jour on est aujourd’hui ? / On est quel jour ? are conversational. Answers include On est lundi, On est mardi aujourd’hui, and Nous sommes vendredi. Starting with on is common, but Aujourd’hui, nous sommes… is also correct.

Weekday names are lowercase except at the beginning of a sentence: Lundi, je travaille (On Monday I work), but Le mardi, je joue au football (On Tuesdays, I play football). Monday is the first day in ISO-style and many French calendars; calendar layouts in Canada and the United States also commonly start on Sunday. The source’s all-calendar claim is not universal.

The complete weekday sequence is lundi, mardi, mercredi, jeudi, vendredi, samedi, dimanche — Monday through Sunday. Match tense to time: Hier, nous étions mardi; demain, nous serons jeudi. À lundi ! is the farewell “See you Monday”, distinct from adding à before an ordinary event’s weekday.

Prepositions, articles and repeated days

For when an action happens, use Je travaille dimanche / Je sors samedi (I’m going out Saturday), without English-style sur or unnecessary à. À dimanche is a farewell meaning “See you Sunday.”

A bare weekday often identifies one occasion: Lundi, je travaille. Le mardi often describes a recurring Tuesday routine. General preferences also use the article: J’aime le samedi parce que je me repose (I like Saturday(s) because I rest), Je déteste le lundi (I hate Monday). J’aime les samedis can also be grammatical when the plural occasions are intended; the source’s singular correction was a preferred practice model, not a ban on plural weekdays.

Tous les samedis means every Saturday. Tout le samedi instead means the whole of a particular Saturday. Thus Je vais au cinéma tous les samedis is the repeated habit; tout le samedi would express duration, not recurrence. Keep tous, les, and samedis plural.

Tous les jours means every day; toute la journée means all day. Tous les lundis has plural tous/lundis. Lundi prochain / lundi dernier identify next/last Monday.

Reflexive verbs and plans

Se reposer means rest: je me repose / je ne me repose pas. Match the reflexive pronoun to its subject. Other models are Je me couche tard (I go to bed late), Je me réveille de bonne heure (I wake up early), and On se promène (we/people take a walk).

Use aller + infinitive for a future intention: Vendredi soir, je vais regarder un film, Je vais rester à la maison, Je vais me reposer (I’m going to rest). For taking a child to an activity, Je vais accompagner mon enfant au cours de karaté is clearer than the source’s English-influenced prendre ma fille au karaté. A generic country example is Des voyageurs vont au Pakistan, with à + le → au.

Habituellement marks a routine: Habituellement, le vendredi soir, une personne travaille sur un projet personnel, puis elle se couche tard. Another generic example is Le vendredi soir, une famille mange habituellement de la pizza et regarde la télévision ou un film.

The reflexive pronouns are je me, tu te, il/elle se, nous nous, vous vous, ils/elles se. Nous nous reposons le dimanche — We rest on Sundays — illustrates plural agreement; before an infinitive use the pronoun matching its understood subject.

Preferences, reasons and a complete role-play

Use aimer, détester, préférer. Ask Quel est ton/votre jour préféré de la semaine ?, Quel jour de la semaine aimes-tu le moins ? (Which day of the week do you like the least?), or Quel jour aimez-vous le moins ?. Le moins means least. The same interview pattern applies to work tasks: Quelle tâche aimez-vous le moins ?

Give a full answer: Je préfère le jeudi et le samedi (I prefer Thursday and Saturday), rather than only Jeudi et samedi when practising sentence construction. Reasons can use parce que + clause:

  • Parce que c’est le dernier / le premier jour de travail de la semaine.
  • Parce que ça annonce le week-end. (Because it signals the weekend)
  • Parce que je peux passer du temps avec ma famille.
  • Parce qu’un enfant ne va pas à la garderie ce jour-là.
  • Une personne aime le moins le mardi parce qu’un membre de sa famille a des cours.

Full generic interview:

— Excusez-moi, quel jour sommes-nous aujourd’hui ?
— Aujourd’hui, nous sommes vendredi.
— Quel est votre jour préféré de la semaine ?
— Je préfère le samedi.
— Pourquoi aimez-vous ce jour ?
— Parce que je peux passer du temps avec ma famille.
— Quel est votre programme pour vendredi soir ?
— Vendredi soir, je vais regarder un film.
— Préférez-vous sortir ou rester à la maison ?
— Je préfère rester à la maison.
— Avec qui passez-vous le week-end ?
— Je passe le week-end avec ma famille et mes amis.
— Merci beaucoup. Bonne journée.
— À vous aussi. Au revoir.

Pronunciation

Practise lundi, mardi, mercredi, particularly mercredi. In quick speech, la fin de semaine may reduce toward fin d’semaine. Unstressed vowels can be reduced in fenêtre, maintenant, samedi; the source incorrectly referred to dropping an r in maintenant, a word containing no r. This is about vowel/syllable reduction, not removing an r.

É is generally a closer /e/ vowel and è a more open /ɛ/. The source’s “under/over 90 degrees” mouth-angle analogy and exaggerated smile were mnemonics, not measurable phonetic rules. This contrast can help with past-tense endings, though regional mergers and variation exist.

Moi contains /wa/; moins adds a nasal vowel in its usual pronunciation. Regional French vowels differ; the claim that Canadian vowels “have not evolved since the nineteenth century” is an oversimplification. Exposure to multiple accents is useful. Practise famille, travaille, fille, where the letter sequence represents a /j/-type sound; this is not a rule for every double l word. Contrast bon with bonne: the latter has a pronounced /n/ and a different vowel. Bonne journée must not be reduced to bon journée in a careful practice answer.

Au revoir is not pronounced by sounding out every letter independently. Aujourd’hui contains the elements au / jour in its history, but is learned as a word. Practise je préfère and the accents in préféré/préférée. Distinguish mon fils (son), ma fille (daughter), mes filles (daughters); their spelling/pronunciation differs.

Regional and cultural vocabulary

L’Action de grâce is Canadian Thanksgiving; a standard greeting is Joyeuse Action de grâce. La fin de semaine is common Canadian French; le week-end is common in France and also understood in Canada. Un anniversaire is a birthday; in Québec, bonne fête commonly means happy birthday, whereas in France fête may refer to a name day or celebration. The source reversed/blurred that regional distinction.

Le film is a movie; le cinéma can mean the cinema building, the art, or the activity of going to movies. Se promener means take a walk; aller au parc only specifies going to the park. La navette is a shuttle; un stationnement complet is a full parking lot. These remain useful vocabulary even though the original actual outing details are excluded.

La déprime / le blues du dimanche soir describes Sunday-evening blues. Quand on aime ce qu’on fait, on n’a plus trop la déprime du dimanche (When you like what you do, you no longer really get the Sunday blues) demonstrates generic on and ne… plus. Ça me donne des frissons (it gives me chills) , mais c’est intéressant is an idiomatic contrast. The source discussed traditional Wednesday school schedules in France; this is not a universal current rule for every age or school. Keep the vocabulary jour de repos, activités, tennis, danse, théâtre, rather than presenting one school schedule as fixed.
